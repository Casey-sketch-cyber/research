## Title

Evaluating Transformable Furniture in Compact Housing: Schedule-Aware Equivalent Floor Area and Implications for Minimum Space Standards

## Abstract

Compact urban dwellings are regulated primarily by floor area, while transformable furniture is widely promoted to expand livable space. Yet area-only rules, single-room prototypes, and layout generators cannot establish how much usable floor area transformable furniture saves across diverse homes and daily routines. Here, we compare each dwelling against itself under static (S0), form-only (S1), and form + time (S2) furniture strategies—arranged by constraint optimization and independently verified—and translate capacity differences into equivalent floor area (m²). Across 2619 compact plans and four household schedules (31,428 verified layouts), most observed plans (61.0%–90.8%) already function with static furniture. Gains concentrate in two-person households at intermediate compact sizes, with median identified positive equivalent-area gains of 6.21 m² for couples and 5.00 m² for parent-and-child households, driven by compact form and daily switching for couples but mainly by form for parent and child. Fixed 45 m² cutoffs disagree with modeled S2 adequacy on 55.7% of couple and 52.7% of parent-and-child observed cases, while daily switching saves 2.24 m² for couples in Hong Kong model shells. Transformable furniture's value is therefore conditional, supporting performance-based supplements to area rules and size-band furniture design.
