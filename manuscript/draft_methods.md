# Methods

## Dwelling sample and area measure

We compared furniture strategies on matched compact dwellings using floor plans from Swiss Dwellings (D1) and ResPlan (D2) and a generated shell grid (D3). The inclusion band was 8–50 m² of net internal area (NIA). We measured NIA as the union of furnishable, storage-only, walkable-only and fixed-service space inside the dwelling. Balconies and walls fall outside this measure. Eligibility required residential use, a habitable room, an entrance, a window, sanitary provision and kitchen provision. After normalizing plan geometry to metres, we removed duplicate geometries within source and retained site groups. D1 sampling was stratified by area bin and room count with a per-site cap. The retained D2 sample used the corpus scale calibration. D3 varied area, aspect ratio, entrance and window positions with a fixed toilet pod and kitchenette. We analyzed the observed plans and generated shells separately. <!-- config/project.yaml; reports/phase_02_corpus.md; results/corpus/counts.csv; results/corpus/d2_scale_audit.csv -->

Before evaluating furnished layouts, we screened each frozen plan on the empty 0.10 m raster. A plan had to provide access from the entrance to the fixed-service targets at the 0.60 m walking requirement. At least half of the furnishable raster had to be in walking coverage or reachable from it within an additional 0.60 m through unobstructed cells. This expansion respected walls and blocked diagonal corners. We excluded a whole plan if it failed the screen and retained independently valid layouts with zero functional coverage. <!-- reports/phase_07_expA.md; results/expA/evaluability.csv; results/expA_f1/acceptance.json -->

## Household demand, furniture and scoring

The four primary households represented a single commuter (H1), a single remote worker (H2), a couple with one remote worker (H3), and a parent and child (H4). Each modeled day had six slots totaling 24 h. The couple's alternate day added a larger dinner demand with frequency 0.143. The older-adult schedule (H5) was a sensitivity case. We assigned furniture-dependent needs for sleeping, eating, work, leisure and storage to slots. Cooking and hygiene were assigned to fixed rooms whose service areas affected geometric feasibility. Every ordinary activity required a distinct compatible item at the time of use. The model credited storage in proportion to reachable capacity, up to demand. The body dimensions and operating clearances came from the reviewed catalogue. Amazon Berkeley Objects listing dimensions provided context for this check. <!-- config/households.yaml; config/furniture.yaml; results/furniture/abo_dimension_check.csv; results/tables/g4/T3.csv -->

S0 used static furniture. S1 admitted transformable items with one state per modeled day. S2 kept item locations and orientations fixed but allowed states to change between slots. For a day variant, the functional capacity index (FCI) divided the served weighted activity-hours by demanded weighted activity-hours. We averaged the variant scores by their configured frequencies. Transformation burden (TB) summed item-specific switching costs across consecutive slots, including the transition back to the first slot, and used the same frequency aggregation. <!-- config/households.yaml; config/furniture.yaml; src/fea/validate.py -->

## Layout search and independent validation

Candidate poses used the dominant plan axis and orthogonal rotations. The search checked containment, furniture-body collisions, operating clearances, anchors, tall-item restrictions near windows, and door keep-outs. For each slot, it checked circulation from the entrance to required use positions and service targets. The CP-SAT search used a greedy starting layout, added constraints for reachability violations, and kept an independently checked incumbent. We prioritized weighted service, then switching burden, with window proximity as a further preference. The validator independently recomputed feasibility, FCI and TB for each selected layout. In the production pipeline, each later strategy could reuse a revalidated earlier-strategy solution. <!-- config/project.yaml; reports/phase_06_pilot.md; results/expA_f1/analysis/independent_example_recheck.csv -->

For NIA at most 20 m², the accepted production configuration searched 0.10 m anchor positions with a 120 s cap per case. Above that size, the anchor stride was 0.20 m and the cap was 30 s. Collision and access calculations used the 0.10 m raster for both groups. After correcting the catalogue inventory to permit two single beds or two single wall beds where needed, we reran the two-person households and verified that the accepted single-person results were unaffected. <!-- results/expA_f1/run.json; results/expA_f1/analysis/configuration_correction_summary.csv; reports/phase_07_F1_F2_results.md -->

## Paired coverage and area estimates

Each unit–household pair supplied a form effect (S1 − S0), a time effect (S2 − S1) and a total effect (S2 − S0). To estimate equivalent area, we fitted the median S0 FCI at each 2 m² NIA bin midpoint for the same household and reference population, applied a cumulative maximum and interpolated linearly. We inverted both scores on that curve at the smallest area attaining each score and subtracted the two implied areas. Equal scores identify zero gain. For unequal scores, an inverse at the lower support boundary or a score beyond the upper supported capacity makes the difference a lower bound. We reported the median among identified positive gains, a lower bound for the median among improved pairs, and bounds on the median over all pairs. The pooled-reference sensitivity re-inverted the same observed-plan scores using a curve that includes the generated shells. <!-- results/expA_f1/analysis/primary_effects.csv; results/expA_f1/analysis_p1/f0_curves.csv; results/expA_f1/analysis_p1/paired_equivalent_area.csv; results/g3/area_summary.csv -->

Adequacy meant FCI ≥ 0.95. We also evaluated 0.90 and 1.00. Minimum adequate area, Aₘᵢₙ, was the within-support crossing at 50% adequate from an unpenalized logistic fit. The contrast was Aₘᵢₙ(S0) − Aₘᵢₙ(S2). We withheld crossings when the fitted probability did not increase, the fit separated, adequacy had one class, or the crossing lay beyond observed NIA support. Paired percentile intervals used 1,000 unit resamples, refitting each area curve or crossing. Area-statistic intervals required at least two contributing units and 80% supported resamples. <!-- config/project.yaml; results/expA_f1/analysis/min_adequate_area.csv; results/expA_f1/analysis_p1/run.json; reports/phase_07_P1_results.md -->

## Benchmark, area comparators and sensitivity

On 200 frozen unit–household pairs balanced across source, area tercile and household, we compared standalone S2 CP-SAT, footprint greedy and simulated annealing under the benchmark settings. Each standalone search used the common candidate and validation rules and started without saved production layouts. The production reference used accepted S2 scores and summed the recorded S0, S1 and S2 solve times. Standalone elapsed time included S2 preparation and validation. <!-- reports/phase_08_B_results.md; results/g3_review/benchmark/algorithm_summary.csv -->

We measured within-bin FCI dispersion with the interquartile range. We compared modeled adequacy with Ulrich's (2025) area benchmarks of 30 m² for a single person and 45 m² for a couple. We applied 45 m² to both two-person schedules and used Hong Kong's 8 m² basic housing unit minimum internal floor area as a separate area-only comparator. Area per person and rooms per person retained their continuous threshold sweeps. Sensitivity runs varied furniture availability, clearance depth, walking width, grid resolution and time cap. ## Geometry-only prediction

We trained an area-only linear model, a histogram gradient-boosted model, a tabular neural network and GraphSAGE on geometry and household inputs. The targets were S0 FCI, S2 FCI and paired equivalent-area gain measured on the D1 training reference. The D1 split held out entire site groups. Model selection used the validation groups. We evaluated the frozen models on held-out D1, D2 and D3. Point-error metrics used identified labels, including exact zero gains, and we checked bound-only predictions against their lower bounds separately. We recorded MAE, RMSE, R², Spearman rank correlation and inference time. The specified ranking criterion was ρ ≥ 0.80 on held-out sites. A post-hoc ROC AUC assessed whether the existing continuous FCI predictions ranked cases above the adequacy threshold. <!-- results/expC/splits.csv; results/expC/metrics.csv; results/expC/predictions.csv; results/g3_review/benchmark/c_adequacy_auc.csv; docs/RESEARCH_PLAN.md -->

## Room comparison

We approximated the 4 × 3 m reference room using the study catalogue and added three 12 m² generated analogues. The reference calculation deducts furniture clearances. Our primary comparison used daytime NIA minus furniture bodies, with service space included where present. We also applied that measure to the two single-person schedules in the main sample and paired the difference in body-free area with the support-aware equivalent-area estimate. <!-- results/expR/source_values.csv; results/expR/reproduction.csv; results/expR/bridge.csv --> [Bu and Che Mat](https://doi.org/10.13189/cea.2026.140514)

## Furniture dimensions and Hong Kong scenario

The reduced kit experiment tested designs for the commuter and couple. We varied wall-bed width, desk width and depth, table width and depth, and wardrobe frontage. FCIq weights service by size-dependent quality. We applied the same quality curves to catalogue and kit furniture, used wardrobe frontage to set storage capacity, and used FCIq ≥ 0.95 for adequacy. We ranked the tested vectors by mean training FCIq across 30 frozen shells per household in the 8–15 m² target band. Each vector retained its best validated layout per shell, with TB breaking FCIq ties. The bespoke comparator chose the best tested vector independently on each training shell. We tested fixed designs on 15 held-out shells and 15 observed plans with NIA ≤20 m² per household. The matched search follow-up gave two reviewed kit vectors the same additional warm-started 120 s S2 optimization per shell as the catalogue. It retained a separate comparison using the best available saved layouts. <!-- config/project.yaml; results/expK/shell_sets.csv; results/g3_review_k3/design_frontier.csv; results/g3_review_k3/paired_effects.csv; results/g3_review_k3/warm_start_s2.csv -->

The Hong Kong scenario calculated adequacy on 30 matched shapes per integer area across 8–20 m² for the commuter, couple and parent with child. We estimated minimum adequate areas with the common quality rule and resampled the shape templates to account for repeated shapes across sizes. The census source provides territory totals. Joint distributions of household size and dwelling area were unavailable for weighting the scenario. <!-- results/g3_review_k3/hk_adequacy_by_size.csv; results/g3_review_k3/hk_scenario_summary.csv; results/scenario_hk/territory_context.csv; reports/schemas/D6.md -->
